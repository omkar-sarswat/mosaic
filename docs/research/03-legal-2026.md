*Inference:* the strongest position for a state deployment is to treat Schedule II as the floor even where a s.17(2) exemption is notified, because a Puttaswamy proportionality challenge will look at actual safeguards, not exemptions.

### Interception
- Final rules notified 6 Dec 2024; drafts from 28 Aug 2024 ([Khaitan & Co](https://www.khaitanco.com/thought-leadership/New-Rules-for-Lawful-Interception-of-Telecommunications), [India Law](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/privacy-india-lawful-interception/), [rules PDF via THC](https://www.thc.nic.in/Central%20Governmental%20Rules/Telecommunications%20(Procedures%20and%20Safeguards%20for%20Lawful%20Interception%20of%20Messages)%20Rules,%202024.pdf)).
- Committee membership is reported differently by different sources (*verify*). There is no judicial or parliamentary oversight, only executive review. That makes **in-system auditability** DRISHTI's main contribution to accountability for intercepted material.

## Courts in 2026: surveillance technology is being tested now

- **Jantar Mantar facial recognition PIL (July 2026).** CPI(M) MP A.A. Rahim challenged Delhi Police's use of facial recognition and biometric surveillance at protest sites under Articles 14, 19 and 21. The petition argues that neither the standing orders nor the Criminal Procedure (Identification) Act, 2022 authorise indiscriminate biometric collection at peaceful assemblies. The Supreme Court agreed to hear it and tagged it with a pending plea. Delhi Police reportedly told the Court its tool captures only persons with serious criminal records. ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/supreme-court-agrees-to-hear-mps-plea-against-police-use-of-facial-recognition-biometric-surveillance-tools-at-protest-sites-545594), [ANI](https://aninews.in/news/national/general-news/pil-in-sc-challenges-delhi-polices-use-of-facial-recognition-ai-surveillance-at-jantar-mantar-protest20260728140154/), [LawBeat](https://lawbeat.in/top-stories/supreme-court-agrees-to-hear-pil-challenging-delhi-polices-facial-recognition-surveillance-at-jantar-mantar-protest-1621910), [LiveLaw on the police affidavit](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/supreme-court-student-protests-delhi-police-affidavit-facial-recognition-software-only-captures-serious-offense-criminals-546263).)
  - *Design consequence:* DRISHTI's refusal to build live facial recognition (red line 2) and its lack of any protest-related purpose are now directly relevant to a live constitutional question. Keep them as hard constraints and say so in pitches.

## Germany in 2026 (the HessenDATA lineage)

- Hesse (2017, "Hessendata"), North Rhine-Westphalia (2019/20, "DAR") and Bavaria (2024, "VeRA") use Palantir. **Baden-Württemberg** amended its police law and begins use in **Q2 2026**; its data-protection commissioner called the tool "highly invasive". **Lower Saxony**'s governing SPD and Greens reject it, citing data protection and US dependence. The Federal Interior Minister was considering nationwide use. ([iamexpat](https://www.iamexpat.de/expat-info/germany-news/palantir-what-us-based-ai-surveillance-system-used-german-police), [Posteo](https://posteo.de/en/news/germany-baden-w%C3%BCrttemberg-approves-use-of-palantir), [Business & Human Rights Centre](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/deutschland-expertinnen-sehen-in-einsatz-der-us-analyseplattform-palantir-bei-der-polizei-eine-gefahr-f%C3%BCr-die-demokratie/), [Yahoo/AFP](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/germany-weighs-nationwide-palantirs-controversial-161014140.html).)
- We did not find a 2026 ruling on the Bavarian constitutional complaint (*open item*).
- *Lesson:* the German debate has moved from *whether* such tools are lawful (2023 ruling) to *whose* tool and *under what law*. Sovereignty and the statutory basis are the two arguments DRISHTI should lead with.
